# Destination

Andaman And Nicobar

# 1. Overview

- Short introduction: The Andaman and Nicobar Islands are a union territory of India, a breathtaking archipelago in the Bay of Bengal, known for its pristine beaches, coral reefs, and dense evergreen forests. It's a popular destination for nature lovers, adventure enthusiasts, and those seeking serene relaxation. The islands offer a unique blend of natural beauty, rich history, and diverse marine life.
- Geography: The archipelago consists of about 572 islands, islets, and rocks, of which only a few are inhabited. They stretch over a length of more than 700 km from north to south. The two main groups of islands are the Andaman Islands and the Nicobar Islands, separated by the Ten Degree Channel. Port Blair is the capital city. The islands are mostly hilly with dense tropical forests and a coastline adorned with sandy beaches.
- Climate: The islands have a tropical monsoon climate, characterized by moderate temperatures throughout the year. Humidity is generally high. There are distinct dry and wet seasons.
- Why people visit: People visit the Andaman and Nicobar Islands for their stunning white-sand beaches, crystal-clear turquoise waters, vibrant coral reefs perfect for snorkeling and scuba diving, historical sites like Cellular Jail, and lush green rainforests. It's an ideal destination for honeymooners, families, and adventure seekers looking for a tropical getaway with opportunities for water sports, island hopping, and exploring unique biodiversity.
- Interesting facts: The islands are home to several indigenous tribes, some of whom maintain a largely uncontacted way of life. Barren Island is India's only active volcano. The islands played a significant role in India's freedom struggle. The pristine marine ecosystem supports a wide variety of flora and fauna, including rare species.

# 2. Quick Facts

- State / Country: Union Territory of India
- Time Zone: Indian Standard Time (IST), UTC+5:30
- Languages: Hindi, English, Bengali, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Nicobarese
- Currency: Indian Rupees (INR)
- Emergency Numbers: Police: 100, Fire: 101, Ambulance: 102 (General), 108 (Emergency Medical Services)
- Airports: Veer Savarkar International Airport (Port Blair) - IATA: IXZ
- Railway Stations: There are no railway stations on the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. Travel between islands is primarily by ferry or inter-island flights.
- Typical Trip Duration: 5-7 days for a comprehensive experience, though shorter (3-4 days) and longer (8-10 days) trips are also common.
- Internet Connectivity: Available in major towns like Port Blair, Havelock, and Neil Island, but can be slower and less reliable than mainland India. Wi-Fi is available in most hotels and cafes.
- Mobile Networks: Airtel, Vodafone Idea (Vi), and BSNL are the major network providers. Connectivity can be patchy in remote areas and during inter-island travel.

# 3. Best Time to Visit

## Winter (October to March)
- Weather: Pleasant and sunny with clear skies and calm seas. Humidity is lower.
- Temperature: Ranges from 20°C to 30°C.
- Crowd levels: High. This is the peak tourist season.
- Pros: Ideal for all water sports, island hopping, sightseeing, and enjoying the beaches. Calm seas are perfect for diving and snorkeling. Festivals like Island Tourism Festival are celebrated.
- Cons: Higher prices for accommodation and flights. Crowded attractions.
- Festivals: Island Tourism Festival (January), Subhash Mela (January), Beach Festival (April).
- Activities: Scuba diving, snorkeling, sea walking, jet skiing, parasailing, trekking, exploring historical sites, island hopping.
- Budget impact: High. Expect increased costs for travel, accommodation, and activities.
- Who should visit: Everyone, including families, honeymooners, adventure enthusiasts, and those seeking a relaxing beach holiday. Ideal for first-time visitors.

## Spring (March to May)
- Weather: Warm and humid, transitioning towards summer. Skies are generally clear but can become hazy.
- Temperature: Ranges from 24°C to 37°C.
- Crowd levels: Moderate to low, as the peak season ends.
- Pros: Fewer crowds, relatively lower prices compared to winter. Still good for water activities early in the season.
- Cons: Can get quite hot and humid, especially towards May. Risk of pre-monsoon showers.
- Festivals: Beach Festival (April).
- Activities: Beach relaxation, swimming, some water sports, exploring historical sites. Best enjoyed in the mornings and evenings.
- Budget impact: Moderate. Accommodation and flight prices begin to drop.
- Who should visit: Budget travelers, those who prefer fewer crowds, and individuals comfortable with warmer weather.

## Monsoon (May to September)
- Weather: Heavy rainfall, high humidity, and occasional strong winds. Seas can be rough.
- Temperature: Ranges from 22°C to 35°C.
- Crowd levels: Low. This is the off-peak season.
- Pros: Lush green landscapes, waterfalls are full, peaceful atmosphere. Significantly lower prices for flights and accommodation. Ideal for nature lovers who enjoy the rain and tranquility.
- Cons: Many water sports activities (especially diving and snorkeling) may be restricted or unavailable due to rough seas and poor visibility. Ferries can be delayed or cancelled. Limited outdoor activities. Increased mosquito presence.
- Festivals: Monsoon Music Festival (variable dates).
- Activities: Indoor activities, exploring local culture, relaxing in resorts, enjoying the scenic beauty of the islands in the rain. Limited sightseeing.
- Budget impact: Low. This is the most budget-friendly time to visit.
- Who should visit: Budget travelers, nature photographers, those seeking solitude and relaxation, and visitors who don't mind the rain and limited water activities.

## Autumn (September to November)
- Weather: Post-monsoon period. Rainfall gradually decreases, skies start to clear, and humidity lessens. Seas become calmer.
- Temperature: Ranges from 22°C to 30°C.
- Crowd levels: Moderate. Gradually increases towards the end of the season as winter approaches.
- Pros: Greener landscapes after the monsoon, pleasant weather. Good time for water sports as conditions improve. Prices are still relatively lower than peak winter.
- Cons: Early autumn might still experience residual monsoon showers. Some activities might still be recovering from the monsoon closure.
- Festivals: Varies, often local community festivals.
- Activities: Island hopping, beaches, some water sports like snorkeling and diving becoming more feasible, trekking.
- Budget impact: Moderate. Prices start to climb towards the end of autumn.
- Who should visit: Travelers looking for a good balance of weather and fewer crowds before the peak season, nature enthusiasts, and those wanting a more budget-friendly option than peak winter.

# 4. How to Reach

## Flights
- Best airport: Veer Savarkar International Airport (Port Blair) - IATA: IXZ. This is the only commercial airport in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.
- Direct flights: Direct flights are available from major Indian cities like Chennai, Kolkata, Bangalore, Hyderabad, Mumbai, and Delhi.
- Typical flight duration: Varies from approximately 2 to 4 hours depending on the origin city.
- Approximate round-trip flight price ranges (Economy class, can vary significantly by season and booking time):
    - Hyderabad: INR 10,000 - 20,000
    - Bangalore: INR 9,000 - 18,000
    - Chennai: INR 7,000 - 15,000
    - Mumbai: INR 12,000 - 25,000
    - Delhi: INR 12,000 - 25,000
    - Kolkata: INR 8,000 - 16,000

## Train
- Major stations: There are no railway lines or stations on the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. The islands are not connected to mainland India by rail.
- Travel duration: Not applicable.
- Typical fares: Not applicable.

## Bus
- Not applicable for inter-island travel from mainland India. Local buses operate within Port Blair and some larger islands.

## Road
- Self-drive: Not applicable for reaching the islands from mainland India. Inter-island travel is primarily by sea or air. Renting cars or two-wheelers for self-drive is possible on some islands like Havelock or Neil Island.

- Example resorts: Sea Shell Havelock, Symphony Palms Beach Resort And Spa, Barefoot at Havelock, Munjoh Ocean Resort, Silver Sand Beach Resort (Havelock).

## Homestays
- Typical nightly cost: INR 1,500 - 4,000
- Recommended areas: Available in Port Blair, some parts of Havelock, and Neil Island, offering a more local experience.
- Example homestays: Various local guesthouses and homestays can be found on booking platforms, offering basic amenities and local hospitality.

## Best areas to stay
- **Port Blair:** Good for connectivity to other islands, historical sites, shopping, and dining options. Offers a mix of budget to mid-range hotels. Ideal for initial arrival and departure days.
- **Havelock Island (Swaraj Dweep):** Famous for Radhanagar Beach and water activities. Offers a wide range of accommodation from hostels to luxury resorts. Best for beach holidays and adventure sports.
- **Neil Island (Shaheed Dweep):** Known for its tranquil atmosphere, natural bridges, and beautiful beaches (Bharatpur, Laxmanpur). Mostly mid-range and budget resorts. Ideal for a peaceful retreat.

## Pros and cons of each locality
- **Port Blair:**
    - Pros: Main entry point, good transport hub, access to Cellular Jail, museums, shopping, diverse food options. Range of accommodation.
    - Cons: Can feel busy like a city, beaches are not as pristine as Havelock/Neil, less 'tropical paradise' feel.
- **Havelock Island:**
    - Pros: Home to world-famous beaches (Radhanagar), excellent for diving, snorkeling, and other water sports. Good range of resorts and dining.
    - Cons: Can be more expensive and crowded during peak season. Requires ferry travel from Port Blair.
- **Neil Island:**
    - Pros: Very peaceful and laid-back, beautiful natural attractions, less commercialized than Havelock. Ideal for relaxation.
    - Cons: Fewer dining and activity options compared to Havelock. Connectivity can be limited. Requires ferry travel.

# 6. Tourist Attractions

## Cellular Jail National Memorial
- Description: A colonial prison built by the British to house political prisoners, particularly Indian freedom fighters. It's a poignant reminder of India's struggle for independence.
- History: Construction began in 1896 and was completed in 1906. Known as 'Kala Pani' (Black Waters), it confined many notable Indian freedom fighters.
- Highlights: Sound and Light Show (evening), Veer Savarkar's cell, gallows, historical exhibits, eternal flame. The architecture of the prison with its seven wings converging at a central tower is significant.
- Time Required: 2-3 hours for the day visit, an additional 1.5 hours for the Sound and Light Show.
- Best Time: Mornings or late afternoons for the day visit. Evenings for the Sound and Light Show (ensure to book tickets in advance).
- Entry Fee: Approximately INR 30 (day visit), INR 50 (Sound and Light Show).
- Opening Hours: 9:00 AM - 12:00 PM and 1:30 PM - 4:45 PM (closed on Mondays and public holidays). Sound and Light show has varying timings, usually two shows in the evening.
- Photography: Permitted in most areas, but flash photography might be restricted in some exhibits. Drones are not allowed.
- Accessibility: Wheelchair accessible to some extent on the ground floor, but upper floors and some narrow passages might be challenging.
- Nearby Attractions: Anthropological Museum, Samudrika Naval Marine Museum, Corbyn's Cove Beach.
- Nearby Restaurants: Various local eateries and cafes are available in Port Blair city center.
- Tips: Attend the Sound and Light Show to understand the history. Hire a local guide for deeper insights. Book Sound and Light Show tickets early.
- Avoid: Visiting during peak midday heat if sensitive to sun. Trying to rush through.
- Family Friendly: Yes, for older children and teenagers who can understand the historical significance. Younger children might find it less engaging.
- Adventure Rating: Low (Historical/Educational)
- Popularity: Very High.

## Radhanagar Beach (Havelock Island)
- Description: Consistently ranked among the best beaches in Asia and the world, famous for its pristine white sands, turquoise waters, and lush green surroundings. It's an ideal spot for swimming and watching sunsets.
- History: A natural formation, its beauty has been preserved due to its protected status and limited commercialization on the immediate beachfront.
- Highlights: Soft white sand, clear blue water, stunning sunsets, calm waves, surrounding lush forest, natural beauty.
- Time Required: 2-4 hours, or a full day for relaxation.
- Best Time: Late afternoon for sunset views. Mornings for swimming and fewer crowds. Best visited during the dry season (October-May).
- Entry Fee: None.
- Opening Hours: Open all day, typically from sunrise to sunset for swimming and relaxation.
- Photography: Highly encouraged, especially for sunsets. Drones may require permission.
- Accessibility: Easily accessible by road from Havelock jetties. A short walk from the parking area to the beach.
- Nearby Attractions: Elephant Beach (requires boat/trek), Kalapathar Beach.
- Nearby Restaurants: Limited shacks and cafes are available near the entrance, offering basic snacks and drinks.
- Tips: Carry water and snacks as options on the beach are limited. Find a spot away from the main crowd. Do not litter. Respect local regulations regarding swimming after dark.
- Avoid: Leaving valuables unattended. Swimming after dark as lifeguards are not present.
- Family Friendly: Yes, very safe for swimming with children due to calm waters.
- Adventure Rating: Low (Relaxation/Nature)
- Popularity: Very High.

## Elephant Beach (Havelock Island)
- Description: Known for its vibrant coral reefs and excellent opportunities for snorkeling and other water sports. The beach is accessible by boat or a short trek through the forest.
- History: Named for the elephants that were once used for logging in the area. Now a popular spot for marine activities.
- Highlights: Snorkeling with colorful fish and corals, sea walking, jet skiing, speed boat rides. White sandy beach.
- Time Required: 3-4 hours, including travel time.
- Best Time: Mornings for clear waters and fewer crowds. Dry season (October-May) for best conditions.
- Entry Fee: No direct entry fee, but boat charges apply (approx. INR 1,000-1,500 per person for a round trip, often including a complimentary snorkeling session).
- Opening Hours: Varies based on boat operations, generally 8:00 AM - 3:00 PM.
- Photography: Permitted. Underwater photography is popular.
- Accessibility: Reached by a 15-20 minute boat ride from Havelock Jetty or a 30-45 minute trek through a muddy forest path. Not ideal for individuals with mobility issues due to boat access or trek.
- Nearby Attractions: Radhanagar Beach, Kalapathar Beach.
- Nearby Restaurants: Limited shacks offering snacks and drinks.
- Tips: Book boat tickets in advance. Wear comfortable shoes if trekking. Carry sunscreen, hats, and change of clothes. Negotiate prices for water sports.
- Avoid: Touching corals or marine life. Littering.
- Family Friendly: Yes, with caution for younger children during boat ride/trek. Older children and teens will love the activities.
- Adventure Rating: Medium (Water Sports/Nature)
- Popularity: High.

## Ross Island (Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose Island)
- Description: Once the administrative headquarters of the British in the Andaman Islands, now in ruins, offering a glimpse into colonial history amidst overgrown structures.
- History: From 1858 to 1941, it served as the capital of the British administration. Abandoned after an earthquake in 1941 and then occupied by the Japanese during WWII. Named Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose Island in 2018.
- Highlights: Historical ruins (Chief Commissioner's House, Presbyterian Church, Government House, Bakery, Press), deer roaming freely, peacocks, historical photo gallery, light and sound show.
- Time Required: 2-3 hours.
- Best Time: Mornings or late afternoons. Dry season (October-May) for pleasant weather.
- Entry Fee: Approximately INR 50.
- Opening Hours: 8:00 AM - 5:00 PM (closed on Wednesdays). Ferry services run frequently.
- Photography: Permitted and encouraged for the scenic ruins.
- Accessibility: Reached by a short ferry ride (15-20 minutes) from Rajiv Gandhi Water Sports Complex, Port Blair. Walking around the island involves some uphill paths and steps, which might be challenging for some.
- Nearby Attractions: North Bay Island, Anthropological Museum, Corbyn's Cove Beach.
- Nearby Restaurants: A small cafeteria on the island for snacks and drinks. More options in Port Blair.
- Tips: Wear comfortable walking shoes. Carry water. Watch out for deer and peacocks. Consider the light and sound show in the evening for historical context.
- Avoid: Feeding the animals. Wandering off marked paths.
- Family Friendly: Yes, historical interest combined with nature (deer) can be appealing.
- Adventure Rating: Low (Historical/Nature)
- Popularity: High.

## North Bay Island
- Description: Famous for its vibrant coral reefs and a wide range of water sports, similar to Elephant Beach but often closer to Port Blair. The iconic lighthouse featured on the INR 20 note is here.
- History: A popular tourist destination, developed for water sports and reef viewing due to its rich marine life.
- Highlights: Snorkeling, scuba diving, sea walk, glass-bottom boat rides, semi-submarine rides, coral reefs, views of the lighthouse.
- Time Required: 3-4 hours, including travel.
- Best Time: Mornings for clearer waters. Dry season (October-May) for optimal conditions.
- Entry Fee: No direct entry fee, but ferry charges apply (approx. INR 700-1,000 for a combined trip with Ross Island). Activities have separate costs.
- Opening Hours: Generally 8:00 AM - 4:00 PM, depending on ferry schedules.
- Photography: Permitted. Underwater photography is very popular.
- Accessibility: Reached by a short ferry ride (30-45 minutes) from Rajiv Gandhi Water Sports Complex, Port Blair. Access to the beach is easy.
- Nearby Attractions: Ross Island, Viper Island.
- Nearby Restaurants: Several shacks offering fresh seafood, snacks, and drinks.
- Tips: Negotiate prices for water sports. Wear appropriate swimwear. Book ferry tickets in advance, especially during peak season.
- Avoid: Touching corals or marine life. Littering. Overpaying for activities without negotiation.
- Family Friendly: Yes, many activities cater to different age groups.
- Adventure Rating: Medium (Water Sports)
- Popularity: High.

## Corbyn's Cove Beach
- Description: A crescent-shaped sandy beach just outside Port Blair, known for its calm waters, coconut palm-fringed shoreline, and proximity to the city.
- History: A natural beach, popular for locals and tourists alike due to its accessibility.
- Highlights: Swimming, sunbathing, water sports like jet skiing and speed boat rides, relaxing, views of Snake Island.
- Time Required: 2-3 hours.
- Best Time: Mornings or late afternoons. Can be enjoyed year-round, but better during dry season.
- Entry Fee: None.
- Opening Hours: Open all day.
- Photography: Permitted.
- Accessibility: Easily reachable by auto-rickshaw or taxi from Port Blair (approx. 8 km).
- Nearby Attractions: Cellular Jail, Anthropological Museum, Science Centre.
- Nearby Restaurants: Peerless Sarovar Portico (hotel restaurant), small shacks and cafes on the beach.
- Tips: Good for a quick beach visit if staying in Port Blair. Can get crowded. Beware of sandflies in some areas.
- Avoid: Swimming in unsupervised areas or after dark. Leaving valuables unattended.
- Family Friendly: Yes, safe for children to swim.
- Adventure Rating: Low (Relaxation/Mild Water Sports)
- Popularity: Medium to High (due to proximity to Port Blair).

## Samudrika Naval Marine Museum
- Description: Run by the Indian Navy, this museum showcases the rich marine life of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands, including corals, shells, and various species of fish.
- History: Established to create awareness about the oceanic environment and to preserve marine biodiversity.
- Highlights: Wide collection of corals, shells, marine artifacts, models of various marine animals, tribal life exhibits, geographical information about the islands.
- Time Required: 1-1.5 hours.
- Best Time: Any time of day. Ideal for escaping the midday sun.
- Entry Fee: Approximately INR 50.
- Opening Hours: 9:00 AM - 12:00 PM and 2:00 PM - 5:00 PM (closed on Mondays).
- Photography: Restricted inside the museum. Check specific rules.
- Accessibility: Easily accessible in Port Blair.
- Nearby Attractions: Anthropological Museum, Cellular Jail.
- Nearby Restaurants: Various options in Port Blair.
- Tips: Read the information boards to learn about the marine ecosystem. Good for children interested in marine life.
- Avoid: Trying to take photos where restricted.
- Family Friendly: Yes, very educational and engaging for children.
- Adventure Rating: Low (Educational/Informative)
- Popularity: Medium.

## Anthropological Museum
- Description: This museum provides insights into the life and culture of the indigenous tribes of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands. It displays artifacts, tools, and photographs.
- History: Established to preserve and exhibit the cultural heritage of the four Negrito tribes (Jarawa, Sentinelese, Great Andamanese, Onge) and two Mongoloid tribes (Nicobarese, Shompen).
- Highlights: Tools, weapons, clothing, and photographs depicting the lives of various Andaman tribes, including the Jarawas and Sentinelese. Information on their customs and traditions.
- Time Required: 1-1.5 hours.
- Best Time: Any time of day. Ideal for escaping the midday sun.
- Entry Fee: Approximately INR 10-20.
- Opening Hours: 9:00 AM - 1:00 PM and 1:30 PM - 4:30 PM (closed on Mondays and public holidays).
- Photography: Restricted inside the museum. Check specific rules.
- Accessibility: Easily accessible in Port Blair.
- Nearby Attractions: Cellular Jail, Samudrika Naval Marine Museum.
- Nearby Restaurants: Various options in Port Blair.
- Tips: Read about the tribes before visiting for better context. Respect the displays and information regarding indigenous cultures.
- Avoid: Trying to take photos where restricted.
- Family Friendly: Yes, educational for older children and adults.
- Adventure Rating: Low (Educational/Cultural)
- Popularity: Medium.

## Chidiya Tapu (Bird Island)
- Description: Located at the southernmost tip of South Andaman, Chidiya Tapu is famous for its breathtaking sunsets and diverse bird species. It also offers opportunities for short treks.
- History: A natural scenic spot, gaining popularity for its natural beauty and birdwatching opportunities.
- Highlights: Sunset views, birdwatching (especially migratory birds), Munda Pahad Beach, short trekking trails, mangrove forests.
- Time Required: 2-3 hours (excluding travel from Port Blair).
- Best Time: Late afternoon for sunset. Early mornings for birdwatching. Dry season (October-May).
- Entry Fee: None.
- Opening Hours: Open all day, but best to visit before sunset. Gates close around 5 PM for entry to some areas.
- Photography: Permitted and encouraged for landscapes and birds.
- Accessibility: Approximately 25 km from Port Blair, reachable by taxi or auto-rickshaw. The road can be winding. A short walk to the sunset point.
- Nearby Attractions: Munda Pahad Beach.
- Nearby Restaurants: Limited small eateries and shacks offering snacks and drinks.
- Tips: Arrive well before sunset to secure a good viewing spot. Carry insect repellent. Wear comfortable shoes if planning a short trek.
- Avoid: Swimming in rough seas if conditions are not ideal. Littering.
- Family Friendly: Yes, enjoyable for families, especially for sunset views.
- Adventure Rating: Low (Nature/Scenic)
- Popularity: High for sunset views.

## Limestone Caves (Baratang Island)
- Description: Formed over millions of years by the deposition of calcium carbonate, these unique natural formations feature stalactites and stalagmites.
- History: Natural geological formations developed over millennia.
- Highlights: Exploring the fascinating cave formations, the journey through dense mangrove creeks, a boat ride, and a trek through the forest.
- Time Required: Full day trip from Port Blair (approx. 8-10 hours including travel). About 1-1.5 hours at the caves and mud volcano itself.
- Best Time: Dry season (October-May) for smoother boat rides and trekking conditions. Monsoons can make the trek muddy.
- Entry Fee: No entry fee for the caves. Boat charges (approx. INR 800-1,000 per person) and Jarawa tribal convoy permit fees (included in tour packages) apply.
- Opening Hours: Convoys to Baratang operate at specific times, usually starting early morning (6:00 AM, 9:00 AM, 12:00 PM) from Jirkatang check post. The caves are open during daylight hours.
- Photography: Permitted. Flash photography might be discouraged inside the caves to preserve formations.
- Accessibility: Involves a drive from Port Blair (approx. 2.5 hours), followed by a mandatory convoy through Jarawa Tribal Reserve, then a ferry crossing, a boat ride through mangroves, and a short trek. Not suitable for individuals with significant mobility issues.
- Nearby Attractions: Mud Volcano (also on Baratang Island), Parrot Island (evening activity).
- Nearby Restaurants: Basic eateries are available in Baratang town.
- Tips: Start early from Port Blair. Carry water and snacks. Be respectful and do not interact with or photograph the Jarawa tribespeople during the convoy. Wear comfortable walking shoes.
- Avoid: Interacting with or photographing the indigenous tribes. Littering. Trying to reach without proper permits/tour arrangements.
- Family Friendly: Yes, for older children and adventurous families who enjoy unique natural formations and a bit of travel adventure.
- Adventure Rating: Medium (Nature/Exploration/Travel Adventure)
- Popularity: Medium to High.

## Mud Volcano (Baratang Island)
- Description: A natural phenomenon where mud, natural gas, and water are ejected from the earth's surface. It's a small, constantly bubbling mound.
- History: Formed by natural geological processes where gas and mud seep out from underground, often associated with tectonic activity.
- Highlights: Observing the bubbling mud, a unique geological feature.
- Time Required: 30-45 minutes. Usually combined with the Limestone Caves trip.
- Best Time: Dry season (October-May).
- Entry Fee: None. Transport costs (part of the Limestone Caves tour).
- Opening Hours: Open during daylight hours.
- Photography: Permitted.
- Accessibility: Requires a short walk uphill from the road. Can be muddy, especially after rain.
- Nearby Attractions: Limestone Caves, Parrot Island.
- Nearby Restaurants: Basic eateries in Baratang town.
- Tips: Wear shoes that can get muddy. Combine with the Limestone Caves visit.
- Avoid: Touching the mud, which can be hot or have an unpleasant smell.
- Family Friendly: Yes, children often find the bubbling mud fascinating.
- Adventure Rating: Low (Nature/Unique Phenomenon)
- Popularity: Medium.

## Mahatma Gandhi Marine National Park (Wandoor)
- Description: A protected area consisting of 15 islands and open sea, dedicated to preserving marine life, including coral reefs, sea turtles, and various fish species. Access is restricted to specific islands like Jolly Buoy and Red Skin.
- History: Established in 1983 under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, to protect the marine ecosystem of the Andaman Islands.
- Highlights: Glass-bottom boat rides, snorkeling, observing diverse coral formations and marine life. Jolly Buoy and Red Skin islands are known for their clear waters and vibrant reefs.
- Time Required: Full day trip from Port Blair (approx. 6-8 hours).
- Best Time: Dry season (October-May) for clear waters and better visibility. Jolly Buoy is generally open for 6 months, and Red Skin for the other 6 months, to allow the reefs to recuperate.
- Entry Fee: Permit fees (approx. INR 50 for Indians, INR 500 for foreigners) plus boat charges (approx. INR 1,000-1,500 per person).
- Opening Hours: Boats typically depart from Wandoor Jetty in the morning (around 8:30 AM - 9:30 AM) and return by afternoon.
- Photography: Permitted. Underwater photography is popular. Drones may require special permission.
- Accessibility: Reached by a 30-minute drive from Port Blair to Wandoor Jetty, followed by a boat ride to Jolly Buoy or Red Skin Island. Beach landing might involve getting your feet wet. Not ideal for individuals with significant mobility issues.
- Nearby Attractions: Wandoor Beach.
- Nearby Restaurants: Limited options at Wandoor Jetty. Carry packed lunch/snacks.
- Tips: Book permits and boat tickets in advance, especially during peak season. Carry sunscreen, hats, and change of clothes. Do not touch corals or collect shells.
- Avoid: Littering. Disturbing marine life.
- Family Friendly: Yes, children enjoy the glass-bottom boat rides and seeing marine life.
- Adventure Rating: Medium (Marine Exploration/Nature)
- Popularity: High.

# 7. Hidden Gems

## Lalaji Bay Beach (Long Island)
- Why locals recommend them: A secluded, pristine beach accessible via a beautiful mangrove walk or a short dinghy ride. It offers tranquility away from the main tourist crowds, with crystal clear waters.
- How to reach: Take a government ferry from Port Blair to Long Island (approx. 3-4 hours). From Long Island Jetty, either trek through a scenic mangrove forest (approx. 1-1.5 hours) or take a small dinghy (approx. 20-30 minutes) to reach Lalaji Bay.
- Time required: Half-day to a full day, including travel to and from Long Island (if not staying there).
- Cost: Ferry ticket from Port Blair to Long Island (approx. INR 300-500). Dinghy ride to Lalaji Bay (approx. INR 200-300 per person round trip, negotiable).

## Guitar Island (near Long Island)
- Why locals recommend them: A uniquely shaped uninhabited island resembling a guitar, known for its pristine beauty and tranquil environment. It's a perfect spot for swimming and relaxing in untouched nature.
- How to reach: From Long Island, you can hire a private boat or join a local tour to visit Guitar Island. It's a short boat ride from Long Island.
- Time required: 2-3 hours for the visit from Long Island.
- Cost: Boat hire from Long Island (variable, approx. INR 1,000-2,000 for a small boat, depending on negotiation and duration).

## Merk Bay Beach (North Passage Island)
- Why locals recommend them: A beautiful, untouched white sand beach on an uninhabited island, ideal for solitude, swimming, and snorkeling in clear waters. It offers a truly serene escape.
- How to reach: Hire a private boat from Long Island or a chartered boat from Port Blair. It's located near North Passage Island, which is a bit further than Long Island.
- Time required: Half-day trip if arranging a special boat, or part of a longer island-hopping tour.
- Cost: Private boat hire can be expensive (INR 5,000+), depending on the origin and duration. Often combined with other nearby islands.

## Mundapahar Beach (Chidiya Tapu)
- Why locals recommend them: While Chidiya Tapu is known for sunsets, Mundapahar is a relatively less crowded beach near the main sunset point, offering good swimming and a quieter atmosphere. There's also a short trek through the forest leading to it.
- How to reach: Located at Chidiya Tapu, approximately 25 km from Port Blair. After reaching the Chidiya Tapu sunset point, a short walk or a small trek through the forest leads to Mundapahar Beach.
- Time required: 1-2 hours for the beach itself, plus travel to Chidiya Tapu.
- Cost: No direct entry fee, just transport cost to Chidiya Tapu (approx. INR 500-800 for a taxi from Port Blair round trip).

## Cinque Island
- Why locals recommend them: A remote, uninhabited island known for its stunning underwater marine life and pristine beaches. It's part of the Mahatma Gandhi Marine National Park and requires special permits, ensuring its untouched beauty. Features a sandbar connecting two islands.
- How to reach: Requires special permission from the Forest Department (which can be challenging to obtain for independent travelers) and a chartered boat from Port Blair. It's a full-day trip.
- Time required: Full day (approx. 8-10 hours).
- Cost: Chartered boat can be very expensive (INR 15,000-25,000+), plus permit fees. Often arranged by dive operators or luxury tour providers.
